# Crocodile Data Analysis & Visualization Dashboard

This repository contains a Power BI project that provides a comprehensive visual analysis of crocodile data. The interactive dashboard explores various facets of crocodile populations, including their habitats, physical characteristics by species, geographical distribution, and growth stages.

##  Dashboard Overview

The Power BI dashboard is composed of several pages, each offering unique insights into the world of crocodiles based on the provided data.

---

### Key Insights & Visualizations

#### 1. Habitat Distribution 

This visualization shows the count of crocodile families by their habitat type. A significant portion of crocodile populations are found in specific aquatic environments.

* The two most common habitats are **Rivers**, accounting for 131 instances (17.31%), and **Swamps**, which host 124 instances (16.38%).
* Other notable habitats include Forest Swamps, Lakes, and Freshwater Wetlands.

#### 2. Species Size Comparison

This visualization ranks different crocodile species by their average observed length and weight, highlighting the diversity in size across the family.

* The **Saltwater Crocodile** is the largest species shown, with an average length of over 4.0 meters and an average weight exceeding 600 kg.
* The **Nile Crocodile** is the second largest species by both average length and weight.
* Species like the West African Dwarf Crocodile are among the smallest observed.

#### 3. Geographical Distribution 

The data reveals the global distribution of recorded crocodile data by country/region.

**Papua New Guinea** has the highest count, representing 9.7% of the records.
**Cuba** accounts for 7.7% of records, and the **Philippines** represents 6.8%.

#### 4. Growth Stages & Development 

The dashboard includes a clear breakdown of average crocodile size based on age class, demonstrating a direct correlation between age and physical dimensions.

**Adults**: Average nearly 3.0 meters in length and have an average weight approaching 200 kg.
**Subadults**: The second largest class in both average length and weight.
**Juveniles & Hatchlings**: Represent the earliest life stages and are progressively smaller in both length and weight.
